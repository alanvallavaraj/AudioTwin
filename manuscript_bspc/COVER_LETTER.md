# Cover letter — Biomedical Signal Processing and Control

Dear Editor,

Please consider the manuscript **“AudioTwin: Closed-Loop Preference-Guided Personalisation of Hearing-Aid Signal Processing with Safety-Constrained Bayesian Optimisation”** for publication as a Full Paper in *Biomedical Signal Processing and Control*.

AudioTwin addresses hearing-aid personalisation as a closed-loop biomedical engineering problem. Audiometric measurements condition the admissible signal-processing space, behavioural pairwise judgements provide sparse feedback, and preferential Bayesian optimisation selects the next device comparison and updates the recommendation. The controlled variables span six hearing-aid DSP dimensions: gain, spectral tilt, compression, noise reduction, speech enhancement and compression speed.

The study uses a frozen evaluation of 128 simulated listeners across three listening contexts and three pre-specified preference families. At the primary 20-query endpoint, qEUBO reduced mean normalised regret from 0.321 for the strengthened static reference to 0.240, a 25.1% reduction, with 94 of 128 listeners improved. A matched random-pair ablation using the same preference model isolates the value of active feedback selection: qEUBO reduced regret by 0.049 after only five queries.

I believe the manuscript fits BSPC's emphasis on personalised signal/data analytics and closed-loop or feedback-controlled biomedical systems, including assistive devices. The paper is deliberately cautious about translation: all listeners are simulated, deterministic feasibility constraints are not medical-device safety certification, and human-listener validation is identified as the next evidential step.

The manuscript is original, is not under consideration elsewhere, and the author declares no competing interests. Code, released data, analysis scripts and protocol records are available for reproducibility through anonymised supplementary material for peer review.

Thank you for your consideration.
